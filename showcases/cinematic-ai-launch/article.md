# Article In. Video Out.

Most teams already have the raw material for a good video. It is sitting in launch notes, product updates, technical blogs, internal memos, and long-form articles.

The hard part is not writing the first draft. The hard part is turning that draft into something people will actually watch.

The old workflow breaks the job into too many pieces. You rewrite the article into a script. You make slides. You hunt for images. You record voiceover. You open a video editor. Then you try to make the final result feel less like a slide deck.

That is where most content loses its shape. The story was clear in the article, but the video becomes a stack of titles, bullet points, and transitions.

`web-video-presentation` treats the article as the beginning of a video pipeline. The article becomes a spoken script. The script becomes beats. Each beat becomes a full-screen visual step. Motion is designed around the idea being explained, not around a generic entrance animation.

The result is still a web page, but it behaves like a recordable video stage. It has a fixed 16:9 canvas, hidden playback controls, audio-aware steps, and a rhythm that can be captured in one take.

This showcase demonstrates the target: a short product-launch style sequence where the article enters the stage, becomes narration, becomes motion, locks to sound cues, and exits as a finished video.
